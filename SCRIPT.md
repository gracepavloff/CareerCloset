# CareerCloset Pitch 2 Script

**Target:** 4:00 pitch, 0:45 instructor question, 0:30 recommendation/transition.

## Four-minute pitch

### 0:00-0:20 | Slide 1
CareerCloset makes professional clothing accessible to college students who need it for interviews and career fairs. Instead of asking a student to buy an outfit they may wear a few times each semester, we offer a curated rental near campus. We are asking for $15,000 to prove one campus can pay.

### 0:20-0:44 | Slide 2
The problem is not whether students understand professional dress. It is that the cost arrives before the opportunity. A suit or several professional outfits can be a serious expense. When the alternative is improvising or skipping an event, clothing becomes an access barrier to internships and full-time jobs.

### 0:44-1:06 | Slide 3
CareerCloset is a simple loop. A student chooses a size and event, reserves an outfit online, picks it up near campus, and returns it afterward. We begin with curated inventory and scheduled pickup windows while we learn which sizes, events, and bundles actually turn.

### 1:06-1:32 | Slide 4
Here is our evidence boundary: CareerCloset is a new concept, with zero completed interviews and paid rentals in this package. We will not call the problem statement traction. The first test is 30 student interviews and a measured prototype. We need to learn whether students will rent, whether the fit is good enough, and whether they return items on time.

### 1:32-1:56 | Slide 5
The planning model has three revenue streams. An individual rental is $29. We assume $8 of cleaning and handling cost, leaving $21 contribution. A semester membership is assumed at $79, and universities or employers could sponsor access. These are assumptions, not observed economics. The round replaces them with rental, return, and sponsor data.

### 1:56-2:18 | Slide 6
We start with one campus, one career center, and one recurring calendar of fairs and interviews. The target campus assumption is 10,000 students. Distribution can come from career centers, student organizations, residence-life channels, and employer partners. The questions are practical: can we maintain fit availability, will students repeat, and will a university sponsor access?

### 2:18-2:42 | Slide 7
We are asking for $15,000 through a proposed SAFE with a $135,000 valuation cap. At the cap, that creates a 10 percent post-money ownership scenario. This is a classroom financing assumption, not an independent valuation or signed term sheet. The ask is intentionally small because demand and inventory utilization are still unproven.

### 2:42-3:08 | Slide 8
We assume zero current cash before financing. We plan on $2,500 of net burn per month, which gives six months of runway after the ask. Six thousand dollars buys initial inventory. Two thousand sets up cleaning and alterations. Four thousand funds the campus pilot and acquisition, and three thousand covers software, support, and contingency. Cash, burn, and runway are all assumptions until actual records exist.

### 3:08-3:35 | Slide 9
The milestone is one paid campus pilot with a repeatable rental loop. In six months, we want 30 interviews, 75 pilot rentals, one paid sponsor or campus, and an 80 percent on-time return target. We expand only if rental contribution is positive, fit satisfaction reaches 85 percent, and returns meet the target. That gives the investor a clear stop-or-go decision.

### 3:35-4:00 | Slide 10
This financing fits because debt requires predictable rental volume, while a large equity round would overprice an untested hypothesis. A small SAFE funds inventory and evidence. CareerCloset makes professional access affordable while giving campuses a measurable career-readiness tool. We are asking you to fund the evidence that earns scale.

## Likely instructor question: “What stops students from damaging or not returning the clothing?”

**45-second response:** The risk is real, so the pilot measures it rather than hiding it. We would use a deposit or card authorization, a short rental window, documented condition at pickup, and a late-return policy. But those controls are still a proposed operating design, not proven results. The milestone requires an 80 percent on-time return rate and positive rental contribution after cleaning and handling. If the economics fail after returns and cleaning, we do not expand inventory.

## Investor recommendation and transition: 30 seconds

Recommend the proposed $15,000 SAFE conditionally. CareerCloset has a focused customer problem and a testable campus wedge, but no right to claim traction yet. Fund the six-month pilot, release expansion only after paid demand, fit satisfaction, contribution, and return milestones are met. “We are funding access and evidence, not pretending either is already proven.”
